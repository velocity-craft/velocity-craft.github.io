Jeter Player Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 9, 2026

This policy covers Jeter Player for Android, published by Velocity Craft (“we”).

Start with what we cannot do

Jeter Player is a video player with a private Vault. Before describing what we protect, here is what we do not claim:

  • Videos on this device are not encrypted by us — and that includes the Vault. Files you move into the Vault are moved, unchanged, into the app's private storage, which the Android operating system isolates from other apps. On a rooted device, or a device someone else can unlock, that isolation can be defeated. Opening the Vault requires your fingerprint, face or PIN, but that is a lock on the app, not encryption of the files.
  • We cannot recover your videos. We hold no copy. If you lose the device, or uninstall the app, without a backup, the files in the Vault are gone.
  • We avoid absolute security claims. Wording of that kind describes nothing you can verify, so we state conditions instead.

Your videos are never sent to us

Jeter Player requires no account, and Velocity Craft runs no server that stores your videos. Library videos are played directly from your device storage. The videos themselves, their file and folder names, what you watch and when, resume positions, bookmarks and their notes, playlists and Vault contents stay on your device unless you choose to move them (see “Where your videos can go” below), and none of them is passed to the advertising or analytics services described next. Crash reports describe a failure in technical terms; Jeter Player does not add video or folder names to them.

There is no sign-in for users. The app contains a hidden developer panel with Google sign-in, used for internal testing. Only when one of our own designated test accounts signs in there is a one-way hash of its email address attached to analytics and crash reports from that device; for anyone else, signing in adds nothing to them.

What is collected, and by whom

This is a separate matter from your videos, and we keep the two descriptions apart on purpose. None of the following contains your videos or their names. Jeter Player shows advertising through Google AdMob and uses Google Firebase for usage analytics (Firebase Analytics), crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics) and remote feature settings (Firebase Remote Config). Together they collect:

The “unsupported format” report contains only technical details — codec and container type, resolution, frame rate, bit depth, audio channels and sample rate, and the decoding setting — never file names, folder names, paths or track titles.

Google Analytics also records some information automatically: device model, Android version, language setting, and session and engagement data such as first open and time in app, together with a random app-instance identifier. Firebase keeps crash reports for 90 days; analytics event data is kept for no more than 14 months.

The following are not collected and never leave your device through us: your videos and photos, their names and folders, what you watch, files and documents, audio, contacts, calendar, messages, personal info, and precise location. Google may estimate an approximate, city-level region from the IP address for ad delivery and analytics reports; in Google Play's Data safety section this is declared as “Approximate location”. Jeter Player does not request location permission and never reads your device's location.

If you buy Jeter Player Lifetime, or while a Plus Pass you unlocked by watching a rewarded ad is active, ads are not shown. The advertising SDK may still start on your device so that the consent setting and ad-free state can be checked. Analytics and crash reporting work the same on every tier.

Where your videos can go, and whether they are encrypted

Any statement about encryption is only true under specific conditions. Here are all the places your videos and library data can be kept or sent:

PathEncrypted?
Library videos in your device storageNot by Jeter Player. They stay where they are; the app only reads them to play them.
Vault files and the library database on this deviceNo. Protected by Android app sandboxing, and the Vault by its fingerprint, face or PIN lock.
Dropbox backupNo. Vault files are uploaded as they are, together with the library database and your settings. Anyone with access to that Dropbox account can open them. Library videos outside the Vault are not uploaded.
Screenshots and screen recordingsNo. Saved as ordinary files in this device's shared Pictures and Movies folders, where other apps with media access can see them. They are not uploaded.
Moving files out of the Vault, and sharing to another appNo. The file goes, as is, back to shared storage or to the app you choose, under that app's policy.
Local network: DLNA, SMB and Wi-Fi transferUsually not. These features talk directly to devices on your own network, mostly over unencrypted connections. Wi-Fi transfer requires a 4-digit PIN shown in the app, shuts down after 5 wrong PINs, and stops after 10 minutes without activity.
Android OS device backup (Auto Backup / device transfer)Not by Jeter Player. If Android backup is on, the library database and Vault files can be included in the backup or in a device-to-device transfer, subject to Android's size limits; Google manages that backup. Jeter Player excludes the Dropbox, Google sign-in and SMB credentials, purchase-state caches and test settings.

Connections to Google and Dropbox use encrypted HTTPS. A web link you share into the app is fetched the way the link itself specifies (http or https).

Dropbox backup

Dropbox backup is optional and is available with a Plus Pass or Jeter Player Lifetime. If you connect Dropbox, authorization happens with Dropbox, and the connection tokens are kept on this device in storage encrypted with the Android Keystore (on the rare device whose Keystore is damaged, in the app's private storage instead). Jeter Player talks directly to Dropbox; Velocity Craft runs no server that receives your videos.

Each backup contains your Vault files (the actual videos and photos, stored once and shared between backups); the library database — the names and folder paths of the videos in your library, playback history and resume positions, bookmarks and their notes, playlists, Vault folders, Auto-Vault rules and your Pulse balance; your settings; and a small unencrypted summary file with the app package, backup time, database version and the number of Vault files. None of it is encrypted by the app. Up to 20 backups are kept; older ones are deleted, while the shared Vault files stay. Dropbox stores these files under your account and can observe service metadata such as file names, sizes, upload times and your network address. Dropbox's handling is governed by Dropbox's privacy policy.

Uninstalling Jeter Player or disconnecting Dropbox does not delete files already in Dropbox. To remove them, delete Jeter Player's folder in your Dropbox and, if you wish, revoke the app in your Dropbox account settings.

Features that contact other services

  • Saving a video from a shared link. When you share a web link into Jeter Player, the app fetches that address to check whether it is a video (on a web page, it looks for a video link the publisher has made public) and, if you save it, downloads the video. The site receives the link and your network address, as it would if you opened the link in a browser. Jeter Player does not download videos from social networks such as YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, X and TikTok; such links are refused.
  • Fonts. Some of the app's built-in typefaces, and any app font you choose, are downloaded from Google Fonts by Google Play services on your device.
  • App updates and language packs are delivered through Google Play (in-app update and Play Feature Delivery).
  • Local network. DLNA servers, SMB shares and Wi-Fi transfer connect only to devices on your own network that you choose; nothing is sent to us. SMB passwords are kept on this device in Keystore-encrypted storage and are excluded from Android backup.

Third parties

  • Google AdMob and the Google User Messaging Platform — advertising and consent. Governed by Google's privacy policy.
  • Google Firebase — Analytics usage events, Crashlytics crash reports, and Remote Config for feature settings. Your videos and their names are never passed to Firebase. Governed by Google's privacy policy.
  • Google Play — Billing for the Lifetime purchase (we never see your payment details), in-app updates and language packs; Google Play services for fonts and, in the hidden developer panel only, Google sign-in.
  • Dropbox — only if you connect an account for backup.
  • Websites whose links you share into Jeter Player — only when you share a link, to check and download the video.

Permissions

  • Photos and videos — to find and play the videos on your device, and to run Auto-Vault rules you set up. On Android 14 and later you can allow access to selected items only.
  • Notifications — playback controls, and the progress of backups and restores, compression, downloads from links, SMB or Wi-Fi transfer, and screen recordings. Jeter Player does not send promotional notifications.
  • Microphone — only for screen recording. On Android 10 and later it captures the sound the app is playing; on Android 8 and 9, where that is not possible, it records from the microphone. Recordings are saved only on this device.
  • Screen capture — for screen recording, which starts only after you confirm Android's screen-capture prompt.
  • Internet, network and Wi-Fi state, and multicast — for ads, analytics, crash reporting, Dropbox backup, saving videos from links, and finding DLNA and SMB devices on your local network.
  • Background work (foreground services for media playback, data sync and connected devices) — so that playback, Picture-in-Picture, backups, downloads and Wi-Fi transfer can continue while the app is in the background.
  • Biometrics — for the Vault. Fingerprint or face data stays with Android; Jeter Player only learns whether the check succeeded.

European Economic Area and the United Kingdom

Where required, Jeter Player presents a Google-certified consent form before any advertising. You can change your choice at any time from Settings. Depending on your choice, ads are shown in non-personalized form or not loaded at all. This form governs advertising only. Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics are not controlled by it and run on every device; Analytics can read the advertising ID, which you can reset or delete in Android settings.

You have the right to access, correct, and delete your data. Your videos and library data are held only on your device and in storage you control, so you exercise those rights directly: delete videos and Vault items in the app, uninstall to remove the Vault and everything else the app keeps in its private storage (screenshots and recordings saved to shared folders stay until you delete them), delete Jeter Player's folder in Dropbox if you used backup, and manage your Android device backup in Google's settings. Analytics, crash and advertising data are held by Google; none of it is linked to your name or email (except our own designated test accounts, described above). For that data, use Google's tools or contact us and we will help where we can. See how to delete your Jeter Player data.

Children

Jeter Player is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.
