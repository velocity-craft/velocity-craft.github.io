Inscape for iPhone and iPad — Privacy Policy

Applies to the iOS and iPadOS app · Updated November 28, 2026

Inscape is a personal journal developed by VelocityCraft. This policy describes the iPhone and iPad app.

Your journal on your device

You can write and read without an Inscape account or an internet connection. Entries and search data are stored in an encrypted database, and imported attachments are encrypted in the local library. The local encryption key is kept in this device’s Keychain. Display and lock preferences are stored locally.

The app can ask Apple’s system authentication to unlock using Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode. It receives the result, not biometric data or your device passcode. An optional separate app passcode is verified locally using a derived record in this device’s Keychain. The interface is covered when inactive; when app lock is enabled, authentication on return follows your lock timeout setting. These protections do not prevent every screenshot or replace your device’s security.

The encrypted library is excluded from ordinary operating-system backup. Create a separate encrypted backup in the app and keep its passphrase safe before changing or erasing your device.

Optional Dropbox sync

If you connect Dropbox, authorization happens directly with Dropbox. Inscape keeps connection credentials and the sync passphrase in this device’s Keychain. It encrypts journal and attachment content before upload and decrypts it on compatible connected Inscape installations using the same sync passphrase. The passphrase is not sent to VelocityCraft or Dropbox. VelocityCraft does not operate a server receiving your journal or encryption keys.

Dropbox stores the encrypted objects in your account and receives service information including paths, sizes, revisions, request times, and your network address. This service information is outside journal-content encryption. See Dropbox’s privacy policy.

Backups, photos, and files

The iOS backup screen creates an encrypted archive with a separate passphrase and lets you choose where to share or save it. Archive structure, sizes, creation times, and encryption metadata can remain visible. A storage service you choose handles the saved archive under its own policies. We cannot recover a lost encryption key or forgotten sync or backup passphrase.

The app reads photos and files you choose through system pickers. It does not request your current location, contacts, or calendar. Selected files and imports can contain metadata you chose to bring into your journal. Files exported by another Inscape edition may have different protection.

Voice input and pasted web links

When you start dictation, the app requests microphone and speech-recognition permission and uses microphone audio for on-device transcription. It requires an available on-device speech model; if that model is unavailable, dictation is unavailable rather than sent to a server. Dictation does not create an audio recording file. The recognized words become part of your local entry. Inscape does not send this dictation audio or text to VelocityCraft or to a remote transcription service.

When you paste a web URL into the editor, Inscape may request that web page directly to obtain its title for a Markdown link. The website and any redirect destination receive the requested URL, your network address and ordinary request information; the URL may contain information you included in it. The surrounding journal text is not included in this request. This request does not pass through a VelocityCraft server. Opening a link or a support/legal page also uses the selected website, whose privacy practices apply.

Optional diagnostics

The App Store release of Inscape for iPhone and iPad (version 1.0.3 on November 28, 2026) does not send crash reports or usage analytics to Firebase. The following applies only to versions with the optional diagnostics setting.

Diagnostics are off by default on each device. If you turn them on in Inscape settings, Google’s Firebase Crashlytics helps us identify crashes and Google Analytics for Firebase helps us measure use of the app. You can keep a journal with this setting off.

  • Crash reports. After a crash, a later launch with diagnostics enabled can send the crash time, stack trace, app state, device model, operating system and app versions, and installation identifiers. The report can include Analytics events leading up to the crash.
  • Usage analytics. Inscape records when you turn diagnostics on and when you start the app with diagnostics already enabled. These two app-defined events have no custom fields. Analytics can also record its own events, such as session start and engagement, along with timestamps, app-instance identifiers, device and version information, language, and approximate region derived from the network address.

Inscape does not send your journal text, entry titles, tags, photos, attachments, filenames, or account and login details as diagnostics. It does not use these reports for ad targeting or to track you across other apps or websites.

You can turn diagnostics off in Inscape settings. This stops new Analytics reporting and requests deletion of pending crash reports. Crashlytics may continue to record crash information locally until you quit the app. Turning diagnostics off does not recall data already sent to Google. For information about processing and retention, see Firebase privacy and security. Apple may separately process App Store, system, and optional diagnostic information under Apple’s privacy policy.

Your choices and support

You can edit entries, move them to Trash, or restore them. Disconnecting Dropbox removes this device’s saved Dropbox credentials and sync passphrase, but does not delete your local library or remote files. You can revoke authorization and manage remote copies in Dropbox separately. Older encrypted objects, deletion records, and previously saved backups may remain; deleting an entry does not erase every historical copy.

If you email us, we receive your email address, message, and attachments and use them to respond. Contact us about deleting support correspondence. Do not send passwords, encryption keys, or private journal content.
