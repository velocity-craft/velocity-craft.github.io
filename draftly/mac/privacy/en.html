Draftly for Mac — Privacy Policy

Applies to the native macOS app · Updated November 13, 2026

Draftly is developed by VelocityCraft. This policy describes the Mac app. Data handling and available features can differ on iPhone, iPad, and Android.

Your notes and files on your Mac

You can write notes without signing in. Notes, imported images and documents, organization information, and settings are stored locally. The app uses the macOS sandbox and accesses files you select for import, export, or backup. Imported files are stored as app-managed copies; editing a note does not edit the original file.

Ordinary notes are not separately encrypted by Draftly. macOS account security, FileVault if enabled, and the protection of your backup locations are separate from Draftly's app lock.

Vault, app lock, and backups

Vault uses a separate local note library and an authentication gate. It does not add separate Draftly encryption to the local database or attachment files. When you use system authentication, Apple handles it and the app receives the result, not your fingerprint or Mac login password. If you set a Draftly app passcode, its verification data is kept locally in Keychain.

If you set a Vault backup passphrase, the Vault portion of a full backup is encrypted. This does not encrypt the ordinary library portion or every exported file. Without a Vault backup passphrase, a local full backup can contain unencrypted Vault records. Review the selected backup options and protect the exported file. We cannot recover a forgotten backup passphrase.

Dropbox backups and limited-access synchronization

From version 1.0.0 (build 7), eligible Plus or Lifetime access also provides manual Dropbox snapshot backups. When you choose Backup Now, Draftly uploads a full backup to your own Dropbox app folder over HTTPS. These snapshots include ordinary notes and attachments. If the backup contains Vault content, its Vault portion and attachments must be encrypted with your Vault backup passphrase before upload; a backup containing unencrypted Vault content is refused. Ordinary library content is not end-to-end encrypted. Choosing a remote backup downloads a temporary copy and asks you to merge or replace local data before restoring. Existing Dropbox backups are not deleted automatically.

In versions that offer operator access, approved testers can sign in with Google to use two-way synchronization. Draftly checks the verified email address and Google account identifier returned by Google. It remembers the account identifier and keeps the sign-in credentials on the device so it can recheck access. Google can record the authorization and use the connection IP address to prevent fraud. Google’s sign-in SDK lists name, email address and phone number for sign-in functionality, and identifiers, usage and other data for functionality or analytics. These are Google’s disclosures for its SDK. Draftly uses only the verified email and account identifier to decide operator access, and does not ask for a phone number. Draftly sends no note content to Google for this check. You can sign out in Draftly and manage the authorization in your Google account. Writing notes and purchasing or restoring Lifetime do not require this sign-in. See Google’s privacy policy.

If you connect Dropbox and enable two-way sync, Draftly exchanges notes, attachments and sync information with your Dropbox account. Dropbox credentials are stored in Keychain. Draftly does not end-to-end encrypt ordinary library content. For synchronized Vault content, the Vault backup encryption setting applies. If no Vault backup passphrase is configured, Dropbox can receive Vault items without Draftly encryption. If a previously configured key is unavailable, the app skips those Vault items.

Image metadata may include precise GPS coordinates already present in a selected photo. Synchronization information also includes a persistent identifier for this app installation and PDF reading positions so another device can resume at the same page. These are used for app functionality, not advertising or tracking across other apps.

Dropbox also receives normal service information such as the network address, request time, and file paths, sizes, and revisions. See the Dropbox Privacy Policy. VelocityCraft does not operate a server that receives your synchronized notes. Disconnecting stops the app's connection; it does not erase copies already stored in Dropbox or elsewhere. Manage remote files and revoke access separately in Dropbox.

Links and optional system services

Link previews may request a pasted web address and related preview resources. The websites receive those requests and normal connection information. The app also requests static configuration from velocitycraft.app and update information from Apple. It does not add note content or account identifiers to those configuration and update requests, but the services can receive an IP address and technical request information.

Where voice input is available, it requires microphone and speech recognition permission. The app requests on-device recognition when supported; otherwise, Apple's speech service may process audio remotely under your system permissions and Apple's policies. Where a template's location variable is available, it uses location permission and Apple's location and geocoding services to insert a place name. Apple may process the location to resolve that name, and the inserted text becomes part of the note. Availability depends on the Mac version and system support. You can manage permissions in System Settings.

Starting with version 1.0.0 (build 8), the public app does not offer a separate Draftly account or Sign in with Apple. Earlier test builds may have saved an Apple identifier, authorized name or email, and credentials locally in Keychain; this update does not automatically erase those records. Draftly does not send that profile to a VelocityCraft account server. Purchases are handled by Apple through StoreKit. Draftly checks verified purchase status on your device and does not receive payment-card details. An optional Dropbox connection is separate from a Draftly account. See Apple’s privacy policy.

Diagnostics, notifications, and sharing

The Mac app does not include an advertising SDK, a separate app analytics service, or an automatic crash-report upload service. Google’s processing for optional operator sign-in is described above. Optional activity logging stays on the device. Apple may separately process App Store and system diagnostics.

Notifications are used for reminders you configure. Images and documents are accessed through your selected import or file access. The current Mac app uses photo selection rather than camera capture. Selected images can contain embedded metadata. Sharing and backup create files in a location you choose. Recipients and storage providers may be able to read their contents depending on the file format and protection you select.

Your choices and support

Delete unwanted content in the app and manage backups, exported files, and cloud copies separately. If you used Sign in with Apple in an earlier test build, you can manage that authorization in your Apple Account settings. That authorization is separate from your notes, StoreKit purchases, and Dropbox connection. Keep a backup you can restore before removing the app or replacing a library.

If you email support, we receive your email address, message, and any attachments you send. The in-app feedback flow prepares a message with technical details such as the app version, macOS version, memory, and language. You can review and remove those details before sending.
