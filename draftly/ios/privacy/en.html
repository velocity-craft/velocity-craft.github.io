Draftly for iPhone and iPad — Privacy Policy

Applies to the iOS and iPadOS app · Updated November 13, 2026

Draftly is developed by VelocityCraft. This policy describes the iPhone and iPad app. Android and other editions may handle data differently.

Notes and settings on your device

You can use Draftly without signing in. Notes, imported images and documents, organization settings, and preferences are stored on your device. Vault uses a separate local note library and an authentication gate. Draftly does not add separate encryption to the ordinary or Vault database and attachment files.

Device authentication is handled by Apple. Draftly receives an authentication result, not your Face ID data, fingerprint, or device passcode. Your device security, exported files, and operating-system backups have their own protections.

If you set a Vault backup passphrase, the Vault portion of a full backup is encrypted. This does not encrypt the ordinary library portion or every exported file. Without a Vault backup passphrase, a local full backup can contain unencrypted Vault records. Review the selected backup options and protect the exported file. We cannot recover a forgotten backup passphrase.

Dropbox backups and limited-access synchronization

From version 1.0.0 (build 7), eligible Plus or Lifetime access also provides manual Dropbox snapshot backups. When you choose Backup Now, Draftly uploads a full backup to your own Dropbox app folder over HTTPS. These snapshots include ordinary notes and attachments. If the backup contains Vault content, its Vault portion and attachments must be encrypted with your Vault backup passphrase before upload; a backup containing unencrypted Vault content is refused. Ordinary library content is not end-to-end encrypted. Choosing a remote backup downloads a temporary copy and asks you to merge or replace local data before restoring. Existing Dropbox backups are not deleted automatically.

In versions that offer operator access, approved testers can sign in with Google to use two-way synchronization. Draftly checks the verified email address and Google account identifier returned by Google. It remembers the account identifier and keeps the sign-in credentials on the device so it can recheck access. Google can record the authorization and use the connection IP address to prevent fraud. Google’s sign-in SDK lists name, email address and phone number for sign-in functionality, and identifiers, usage and other data for functionality or analytics. These are Google’s disclosures for its SDK. Draftly uses only the verified email and account identifier to decide operator access, and does not ask for a phone number. Draftly sends no note content to Google for this check. You can sign out in Draftly and manage the authorization in your Google account. Writing notes and purchasing or restoring Lifetime do not require this sign-in. See Google’s privacy policy.

If you connect Dropbox and enable two-way sync, Draftly exchanges notes, attachments and sync information with your Dropbox account. Dropbox credentials are stored in Keychain. Draftly does not end-to-end encrypt ordinary library content. For synchronized Vault content, the Vault backup encryption setting applies. If no Vault backup passphrase is configured, Dropbox can receive Vault items without Draftly encryption. If a previously configured key is unavailable, the app skips those Vault items. Dropbox also receives service information such as your network address, request times, file paths, sizes, and revisions. See Dropbox’s privacy policy.

Synced image metadata can include precise GPS coordinates read from the selected photo. Sync information also includes a persistent identifier for this app installation and a PDF reading position so another device can resume on the same page. These fields support sync and reading continuity, not advertising or cross-app tracking.

VelocityCraft does not operate a server that receives these synced notes. Disconnecting stops the app’s connection; it does not erase copies already saved in Dropbox or exported elsewhere. Manage remote files and revoke authorization in Dropbox separately.

Speech, links, and system services

Voice input requires microphone and speech-recognition permission. Draftly requests on-device recognition when supported. Otherwise Apple’s Speech service may process audio remotely under your system permission and Apple’s policies. You can revoke permissions in Settings.

If you use a location variable in a template, the app asks for location permission and uses Apple’s location and geocoding services to insert an area name. Apple’s geocoding service may process the location to resolve that name. The inserted text becomes part of your note. Location access can be revoked in Settings.

Link previews can request the URL you paste and associated preview resources. The destination website receives that request and ordinary connection information. Draftly also requests its static configuration from velocitycraft.app and app-update information from Apple; the app does not add note content or an account identifier to those requests. Network services can receive your IP address and technical request information.

Starting with version 1.0.0 (build 8), the public app does not offer a separate Draftly account or Sign in with Apple. Earlier test builds may have saved an Apple identifier, authorized name or email, and credentials locally in Keychain; this update does not automatically erase those records. Draftly does not send that profile to a VelocityCraft account server. Purchases are handled by Apple through StoreKit. Draftly checks verified purchase status on your device and does not receive payment-card details. An optional Dropbox connection is separate from a Draftly account. See Apple’s privacy policy.

Diagnostics, permissions, and exports

The iOS app uses Google AdMob and Google’s User Messaging Platform. Free users may see a banner at the bottom of eligible note lists. Separately, choosing to watch a rewarded ad can earn Pulse for temporary Plus access. Lifetime remains a separate Apple purchase. We request non-personalized ads, disable publisher first-party ID, and do not request App Tracking Transparency permission or send note content, photo metadata, precise location, or account-profile details to the advertising SDK.

When this feature is enabled, Google’s consent service may refresh regional privacy requirements when the app opens, before a banner or rewarded ad is requested. It may process approximate location and basic app-interaction or performance information to show the applicable privacy choices.

Before requesting an ad, the app checks the consent status provided by Google. Where required, you can review your choices before either ad format is requested and reopen them from Advertising privacy in Settings. Privacy choices themselves do not earn Pulse. A reward is granted only after an available rewarded ad is completed. You can continue using free notes or choose Lifetime.

Google’s advertising SDK may process IP addresses (which can indicate approximate location), app- or device-related identifiers, ads viewed and ad interactions, crash information, performance data, and other diagnostic information to serve ads, prevent fraud, and measure and improve advertising. Non-personalized ads do not mean no data is processed. Google handles this information under its privacy policy; see Google’s SDK data disclosure. Draftly does not operate an advertising-profile server or send its local activity log to Google. Apple may separately process App Store and system diagnostics.

Photos and files are accessed for imports or exports you choose; camera access supports image capture, microphone access supports voice input, and notifications support reminders. Imported photos may contain metadata supplied with the selected image. Backup and share actions create files at the destination you choose. Recipients and storage services you select can access the exported content according to its format and protection.

Your choices and support

Delete unwanted content in the app and manage backup, export, and cloud copies separately. If you used Sign in with Apple in an earlier test build, you can manage that authorization in your Apple Account settings. That authorization is separate from your notes, StoreKit purchases, and Dropbox connection. A lost Vault or backup secret may prevent recovery, so keep a safe backup.

If you send support email, we receive your address, message, and attachments. In-app feedback prepares a draft containing technical details such as app version, device model/name, operating system, screen, memory, and language. Review or remove those details before sending. We use support correspondence to respond to your request. Contact us about deleting that correspondence. Do not send passwords, keys, or private notes.
